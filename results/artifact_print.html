§1The problem prior work mis-measures

Translating a harmful prompt into a lower-resource language is a well-known jailbreak. But almost every cross-lingual study counts "the model didn't refuse" as "the attack worked" — and for exactly the languages that matter, that is wrong. When a small model is asked something harmful in Urdu and emits garbled or echoed text, it wasn't jailbroken; it just didn't understand. Scoring that as success overstates weak models and hides the real mechanism.

The fix
A three-way judge — REFUSE / COMPLY / EVADE — and a comprehension-controlled attack-success rate (cc-ASR): jailbreak success measured only over requests the model actually understood.

§2Safety is alignment-anchored, and comprehension hides the hole

Safety holds in the languages a model was actually aligned in and collapses elsewhere. It is not a clean "high-resource = safe" rule: two effects stack — safety fails to transfer, and where the model half-understands the language (Bangla, Urdu) the raw rate hides how bad it is, because among requests it does understand, it complies almost always.

The sharpest evidence — safety follows alignment, not resource
SmolLM2 (English-centric) is safe only in English and fails in every other language — including high-resource Spanish, French, and Chinese (0.73–0.80). Multilingual Qwen keeps safety in English, Spanish, French, and Arabic (0.01–0.13) but not low-resource ones. And Chinese is a warning: high-resource and fluently understood, yet cc-ASR 0.51 (0.88 on Qwen-3B) — the models parse Chinese but were never safety-tuned in it. Capability is not safety.

§3Safety is entangled with capability

Across all 42 model×language cells, the less a model understands a language, the worse its safety fails on the part it does understand: comprehension and cc-ASR correlate r = −0.47. Cross-lingual safety is not a separate alignment axis — it is a shadow of where the model was aligned. This finding survived a caught bug: human-labeling the Chinese responses exposed a tokenization error that had made Chinese look artificially safe (cc-ASR 0.27 → 0.51 once fixed) — see the honesty panel.

§4The full attack ladder

Placing language-switching on the same axis as syntactic and framing attacks lets us rank them directly. Translation is a top-tier attack — translate_hi and translate_hi+refusal_suppression sit alongside the strongest English tricks (prefix_injection, hypothetical). Obfuscations like base64 are weakest, because small models can't decode them — a comprehension failure the three-way judge correctly scores as EVADE, not success.

Robustness is not monotonic in model size: Qwen2.5-1.5B is far more robust than the larger Qwen2.5-3B, because greater capability improves comprehension of the attack — removing the EVADE "shield" and turning near-misses into genuine jailbreaks.

§5A partial defense — and the real fix

A hardened defensive system prompt ("refuse regardless of language, fiction, or encoding") cuts attack success by only 15–18%, at a real cost in over-refusing benign requests. A prompt can't repair a gap that lives in the weights. The decisive lever is language normalization: routing the request through English before the safety decision would replace an Urdu/Bangla cc-ASR of 0.79–0.86 with the English 0.31 — recovering ~0.45–0.55 of absolute attack success, model-agnostically.

§6What this is — and isn't

  • Validated judge — and it mattered. 0.84 accuracy on 114 hand-labeled responses across all 7 languages; refusals 39/41. Labeling the Chinese responses caught a CJK-tokenization bug that had made Chinese look artificially safe (cc-ASR 0.27 → 0.51 once fixed) — a naive pipeline would have shipped the wrong conclusion. On low-resource languages the residual error over-counts COMPLY, so the reported gap there is conservative.
  • Small open models. Their baseline safety is weak; absolute rates may not transfer to frontier models. The methodological claim — control for comprehension — is model-agnostic, and the harness ships an OpenAI/Anthropic adapter to test frontier models.
  • Translations. 188 cells (the four focus languages) are hand-authored and native-reviewed; 862 more are NLLB-200 machine translations, provenance-tracked and open to review.
  • Ethics. Harmful prompts are short, non-operational refusal triggers. The harness never emits harmful content in shared outputs; successful completions are redacted. The purpose is defensive: reducing a safety gap that disadvantages non-English speakers.
